## The long version

Written in one go on the sofa at ten at night, with my legs up on the arm of it, so forgive the length: the day started in the dark in the lakeside car park, where forty-one of us stood around in reflective vests pretending we were not nervous, and then somebody said go and nobody went for about a minute, which is the most brevet thing that has ever happened. The full account, with the splits and the weather at every control, is in the [ride report](../../hobbies/cycling/rides/202609131900-300k-brevet.md), so this is only the diary version. The first hundred kilometres were easy, mostly because I did exactly what I had told myself to do, which was ride slower than I wanted and eat earlier than I wanted. The first control was a bakery with a queue out of the door and a woman behind the counter who stamped the cards without looking up, which I found oddly moving. The second control, at 171 km, was the one that mattered; I arrived at 13:20, which was forty minutes inside my plan, and promptly lost thirty of them sitting on a kerb eating a sandwich and not wanting to stand up. The stretch after that, the 67 km to the last control, is the stretch I will remember: a headwind that arrived at exactly the wrong moment, a village where every shop was shut for a funeral, and a man outside a garage who filled my bottles without being asked and refused to take any money for it. I was angry at the road for about an hour, and then I was not angry anymore, and then I was a bit emotional about the man with the hose. The last 30 km, from the upper lake, I rode with two others who had also decided the clock no longer mattered, and we finished together at 19:41, which is after the sun went down and well inside the limit. I have written the same sentence three ways already and it still does not say how good it felt to hand the card to the volunteer at the finish, so I will just say it: it was good. Everything in the [ride report](../../hobbies/cycling/rides/202609131900-300k-brevet.md) is true, and everything in this paragraph is the part that does not fit in a table. Details I will want later, when I am planning the next one: eat the second sandwich before the kerb, not on it; carry two spare tubes, not one, because the one I used was the one I had; and ask somebody to look at the [ride report](../../hobbies/cycling/rides/202609131900-300k-brevet.md) before I post it, because I always get the control times wrong when I am tired and happy.
