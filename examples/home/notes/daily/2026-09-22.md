# Tuesday 22 September: the sync queue stalled

A week after the feature freeze, on the day the 10% rollout of sharing started, the sync queue stalled in production for
about two and a half hours. Nothing was lost, because the queue is durable, but nothing moved either, and every offline
edit made by a rider in that window sat on their phone until it ended. These are my notes from the day, written as it
happened and tidied at night. The formal review is on Thursday: [the incident
review](../meetings/2026-09-24-sync-queue-incident-review.md) will have its own write-up; this note is the raw material.

[Malik](../people/malik-haddad.md) ran the incident. I was the second pair of eyes and the person who knew which part of
the code to read.

## Timeline notes

### 14:05

Jo's message in the support channel, copied as it came: "three customers say their rides from the weekend are not on
the new phone. Offline mode, they say, and then they signed in." That is exactly the sync queue stalled symptom, seen
from the rider's side, and it is the first time anyone outside the engineering team noticed.

### 14:05

Malik opened the incident channel in the same minute. We did not know yet that the two were related; I wrote them both
down under the same time because it was the same time.

### 14:20

Malik found the stuck message. The worker had been retrying it on a doubling backoff, and by the time he looked the
backoff had grown past ten minutes, so the worker was asleep and not failing, which is why nothing alerted on errors.

### 15:31

I skipped the message by hand. Not a fix, a way of making the queue move again while we worked out what to fix.

The query I ran to find the message at the head of the queue:

```sql
-- the head of the queue: oldest unprocessed op per user
SELECT user_id,
       MIN(created_at) AS oldest,   -- the stuck one is days younger than it looks
       COUNT(*)        AS pending
FROM sync_ops
WHERE processed_at IS NULL
GROUP BY user_id
ORDER BY oldest
LIMIT 20;
/* then, for the one message: */
UPDATE sync_ops SET processed_at = now() WHERE id = '7f3a'; -- skipped by hand at 15:31
```

The one-liner I pasted in the channel, so nobody had to type the pod name:
``kubectl logs `kubectl get pod -l app=kestrel-sync-worker -o name | head -1` `` and the one for the rollout,
`kubectl rollout status deployment/kestrel-sync-worker`.

The change I think we want, as a diff:

```diff
--- a/api/kestrel_api/sync.py
+++ b/api/kestrel_api/sync.py
@@ -91,4 +91,5 @@ class Sync:
 
     def retry_after(self, attempt):
-        return (2**attempt) * 0.5
+        # mirrors the client: min(60s, 2^attempt * 500ms)  -- see retry.ts
+        return min(60.0, (2**attempt) * 0.5)
 
```

The line in question is [sync.py line 94](../../work/kestrel/api/kestrel_api/sync.py#L94), where the delay is computed,
and the worker's own config is [the worker config](slipbox://infra/k8s/workers/sync-worker.yaml) if you want to see its
limits.
The log from the worker, with the 612 s and 1224 s lines, is in
[the worker log](../../Downloads/sync-worker-2026-09-22.log.md#L120).

## The runbook

We followed [the sync runbook](../../work/kestrel/docs/runbook-sync.md) most of the way, and then the runbook ran out:
it says to restart the workers and nothing about a message that survives a restart. Aiko is moving it into the
`runbooks/` folder tomorrow and adding a section on poison messages; Malik's draft of the new one will live at
[~/work/kestrel/docs/runbooks/sync-queue.md](~/work/kestrel/docs/runbooks/sync-queue.md) once it is merged.

## What Malik said

> Restart is not a fix. Restart is a coin you flip, and the coin was heads once and tails every time after.

> It is a queue. A queue that cannot skip a message is a pipe, and pipes burst.

I wrote both on a sticky note and put it above the monitor.
