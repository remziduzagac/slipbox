Notes on Maps and Territories
=============================

Notes on P. Verhoeven's history of projections, from the [reading list](list.md). I read it because Kestrel draws maps
all day and none of us can say why the northern tiles look stretched. The notes follow the book's chapters, with
footnotes for page numbers, which is my habit; the cite is in the footnote and the opinion is in the text.[^pg]

## Projections[^1]

Every flat map lies about something. Mercator keeps angles and lies about area; the equal-area projections keep area
and lie about shape. The book's point is that the choice is political before it is mathematical.[^2] Our tiles use
Web Mercator, so Greenland looks the size of Africa and nobody on the team has cared until the heatmap.[^3]

I link to this heading from elsewhere as [the projections section](#projections1), with the footnote mark folded into
the slug, and from the middle of a sentence as [Projections](#projections), which should find the same heading.

### Mercator in one paragraph

Mercator's 1569 chart was made for navigators: a constant compass bearing is a straight line on it. That is the whole
trick and the whole defect.[^4]

#### The scale problem

The scale of a Mercator map grows with latitude as the secant of the latitude, so at 60 degrees it is doubled and at 80
it is nearly six times.[^5] <a id="scale"></a>The ruler on a tile at 60 degrees north is therefore half the true length
it claims, which is why our distance badge lies in Helsinki. See [the scale](#scale) for where the book says so.

## Scale and generalisation {#custom-id}

A map at one scale cannot be a map at another; generalisation is what you do instead, and every choice is an
editorial one. The book's example is the coastline of a small island, which is a different length at every scale.[^6]
Heading with an id of its own: [go there](#custom-id).

## What a legend owes the reader

A table of the book's recurring conventions, with the notes where I disagree:

| Convention | The book says | I think[^7] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| north up | an accident of history | fine for a bike map |
| blue for water | nearly universal | also for rivers |
| red for danger | cultural | not on a cycling map; use hatching[^8] |

> A projection is an argument about what the map is for.[^9]

## What I keep

The summary for people who will not read the book: a map is a decision, the projection is the first one, and the scale
is the second; everything else is typography.
