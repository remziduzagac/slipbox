# The Long Ride

D. Okoro's memoir of a year of brevets, from 200 km to the 1,200 that gives the book its end. I read it between my own
300 km on 2026-09-13 and the day I could sit down again; my ride is written up in the cycling notebook as
[the 300k brevet](../../hobbies/cycling/rides/202609131900-300k-brevet.md).
When I come back to it I start from its own [opening](the-long-ride.md#the-long-ride).

## The first night

She rides the first night alone, in rain, and writes that the hardest thing was not the cold but the decision to stop
being proud about stopping.[^1] I underlined that. The first control is a petrol station and the cashier stamps her card
without looking up.[^dup]

[^1]: Chapter 2, p. 38. The line is on p. 39 in the e-book.

## Food and doubt

The book is honest about food: rice, eggs, soup, flat cola. On the second day she cannot eat at all, and rides on
coffee and boiled potatoes for eleven hours.[^2] A reader who has been there will recognise the next paragraph, in
which she apologises to her bicycle.[^ghost]

[^2]: Chapter 4, p. 81.

I wrote that page reference straight after the paragraph, on the train home, and never moved it to the end.

## Randonneur arithmetic

Speeds are slow on purpose. Her average over 1,200 km is 19.4 km/h, with 62 hours of riding and 22 of stopping, which
is why the stopping is the book's real subject.[^2b]

[^2b]: Chapter 6, p. 117; the splits are in the table on p. 118.

## The stamp, twice

The detail of the card stamped without a glance comes back near the end of the book, when she is the one who stamps a
younger rider's card at a control, and I think it is the passage the whole book was written to reach.[^dup]

## What I will steal

- Ride at a pace where she could talk, not breathe.
- Stop before the stop is needed.
- Write the card at the control, not the next morning.
